# Choosing an image for a story or event

Every published story and event gets one main image. An editor picks it today; an agent that
suggests one follows the same rules. If no photo meets all of them, leave the image empty and say
so in the review notes. A missing image is better than a wrong one.

## It must be real and free to use

- **A real photo.** Never an AI-generated or drawn image, even a realistic one: readers take a
  photo next to local news as a picture of the real thing.
- **Free to use, with a license we can name.** CC0, public domain, CC BY or CC BY-SA, for example
  from Wikimedia Commons, or our own photo, or one used with written permission. Check the license
  on the file's own page, not on a page that reuses it.
- **Never copied from the source.** News sites and press releases often show press or agency photos
  ("courtesy of", AP, Getty, a company's media kit). Those are not free, even when the article is.

## It must show the topic, as close to home as possible

- It signals what the story is about at a glance: the bus for a fare change, the road for a road
  project, the building or room for a zoning rule.
- Prefer, in order: the exact place (Sprecher East, the street or interchange named); elsewhere in
  Madison; Wisconsin; anywhere else.
- It must not suggest something the story doesn't say: no company logo or named site when the story
  isn't about that company or site.
- Prefer a recent photo. Buses, signs and buildings change; an old one can look wrong to neighbors.

## It must look good

- **Level and straight.** The horizon and floor are level and vertical lines (walls, poles, racks,
  doorways) stand straight. No tilted, crooked or Dutch-angle shots, and no wide-angle shots where
  the sides lean in or out. A straight-on, centered view (for example looking down the middle of an
  aisle or a road) is best.
- **Sharp and well exposed.** Not blurry, not shot through a car window, not washed out, not
  oversaturated.
- **Clear subject.** One obvious subject, not clutter. Landscape, and at least 1920 px wide (the
  largest size the site makes), so it stays sharp as a full-width hero.

## Credit it

Fill in `alt` (what the photo shows, for screen readers), `credit` (the author's name as the
license asks for it, for example "Jane Doe"), `license`, and `sourceUrl` (the file's page, for
example on Wikimedia Commons) unless it's our own photo. CC BY and CC BY-SA require the credit to be
visible on the page where the photo appears; the site doesn't show it yet (#405).
